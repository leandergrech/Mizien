# Claim Check 002: Comino tree compensation

**Status:** draft for maintainer review
**Verdict:** Largely supported (moderate confidence)
**Cut-off:** 3 October 2026

## The claim

On 11 August 2026, the Environment and Resources Authority (ERA) said 54 protected trees were to be removed and 540 new indigenous trees planted as compensation. Its account also described 624 non-protected specimens and 348 trees to be transplanted. The 7 August clarification said that 92% of the 678 specimens scheduled for removal were not protected.

The short factual claim is supported by ERA's own published figures. The word *compensation* needs a limit: a ratio of new trees to removed specimens records an obligation, not a measured ecological replacement. The proposed works, planting plan and transplant results had not been independently verified as completed by this check's cut-off.

## Rechecking the arithmetic

| Item | ERA figure | Recalculation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Non-protected specimens scheduled for removal | 624 | — |
| Protected trees scheduled for removal | 54 | — |
| Total removal group | 678 | 624 + 54 = 678 |
| Non-protected share | 92% | 624 / 678 = 92.04%, rounds to 92% |
| New indigenous trees for protected removals | 540 | 54 × 10 = 540 |
| Trees to be transplanted | 348 | Separate from the 678 removal group |
| Protected trees among transplants | 341 | ERA says all but seven of 348 are protected |

These are planned interventions and stated conditions, not counts of completed work. Transplanted trees that do not survive are subject to a further 10:1 replacement condition. The calculation file and source inputs are in `data/cc-002/`.

### What “92% not protected” says

It describes a protection classification, not an ecological score. ERA's 11 August statement says the non-protected group was planted for the former hotel's landscaping and that 467 of the specimens were oleanders. A MaltaToday report quoting an ERA case-officer report says 468 oleanders, one more. The available published summaries do not resolve that one-specimen difference; we do not use the species split to estimate ecological loss.

Likewise, the NGO's earlier “around 800 trees” phrase is a rounded campaign description reported by MaltaToday, not an inventory with a published counting method. It is not directly comparable with ERA's later, categorised count of 678 scheduled for removal plus 348 scheduled for transplant. Those categories should not be added together as if all were removals.

## What the science says about replacement

Comino's recent vascular-flora inventory combines historical records with 26 field surveys conducted between 2008 and 2025. It records 490 vascular plant taxa, including 58 strictly protected species and 21 endemic or subendemic taxa. It also distinguishes alien species that are effectively invasive on Comino from alien species that are simply present. This makes local species identity and habitat context material: “not protected” and “alien” do not by themselves mean ecologically irrelevant.

Transplantation can preserve individual plants, but a survival count cannot be assumed from the number moved. A Mediterranean review of 178 plant-translocation cases found that outcomes depended on factors including source material, site suitability, preparation, planting method and aftercare. Its records cover diverse species and actions, not a single survival rate for the trees on Comino.

A field study of mature olive trees followed eight transplants and eight control trees for two years. The transplants were heavily pruned, irrigated and fertilised, yet still showed substantial physiological stress. This small study is not a forecast for Comino's tamarisks, mastic, palms, olives or other listed specimens; it shows why “transplanted” cannot be used as a synonym for “survived without ecological cost”.

Restoration can benefit native communities when alien vegetation is removed, but effects depend on what is removed and what follows. A controlled Azores study found later gains in several native-seed and food-web indicators, alongside immediate disturbance and some responses favouring alien seedlings. It does not establish that removal of any particular Comino specimen will restore habitat.

## Verdict

**Largely supported (moderate confidence).** The arithmetic and intervention categories in ERA's public account are consistent: 624 + 54 = 678; the non-protected share rounds to 92%; and ten new indigenous trees for each of 54 protected removals gives 540. The separate transplant group contains 348 specimens, of which ERA says 341 are protected.

That supports the claim as a description of ERA's announced plan. It does not establish that the planting and transplants will offset ecological impacts. No site-specific before-and-after evidence, transplant survival results, or measured habitat recovery was available for this check. The verdict is not an assessment of the permit's legal merits or tribunal proceedings.

## Evidence that would strengthen the ecological conclusion

- The complete species-level schedule, tree condition and size records, and the final planting and transplant method statements.
- The approved compensation plan, with species, local provenance, recipient habitats, planting sizes and aftercare responsibilities.
- A published survival and condition record for transplanted specimens and new plantings at years 1, 3 and 5, including replacements.
- Site-level vegetation and habitat monitoring that measures native species and ecological function, rather than planting counts alone.
- A reconciled official species breakdown for the 624 non-protected specimens, including the 467/468 oleander discrepancy.

**Limitations:** The direct ERA release supports the planned counts, but the full permit annex and site-specific methods were not available for this scientific assessment. Peer-reviewed evidence is relevant to mechanisms, not a substitute for monitoring these Comino trees.
