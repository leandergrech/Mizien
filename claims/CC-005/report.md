# Claim Check 005: “92% excellent” bathing water

**Status:** Draft for maintainer review  
**Verdict:** Supported, high confidence (for the 2023 statistic quoted by the Commission)  
**Claim source:** European Commission, *Environmental Implementation Review 2025: Malta*  
**Reference year:** 2023

## The claim

The Commission’s 2025 Malta factsheet says: “Malta also shows excellent records under the Bathing Water Directive: 92% of Maltese bathing waters are of excellent quality.” The sentence is accurate for the 2023 bathing season: 80 of Malta’s 87 coastal bathing-water sites were classified excellent (92.0%) by the EEA. The factsheet does not state the year alongside the percentage.

## What the indicator measures

The classification is based on two microbiological parameters: *Escherichia coli* and intestinal enterococci. The EEA applies the Bathing Water Directive’s thresholds to monitoring data over a four-season assessment period. It is a useful indicator of bathing-water quality for the parameters measured. It does not by itself describe every short-lived pollution event, all contaminants, or the ecological and chemical condition of Malta’s marine waters.

## What later seasons show

| Season | Excellent | Other classes | Total sites | Samples |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 80 (92.0%) | 3 good; 4 sufficient | 87 | — |
| 2024 | 80 (92.0%) | 3 good; 4 sufficient | 87 | 2,107 |
| 2025 | 77 (88.5%) | 8 good; 2 sufficient | 87 | 2,100 |

No sites were classified poor in those seasons. All sites in 2025 met at least the directive’s minimum “sufficient” class. The 92% statement is therefore accurate for the year it reflects, but should not be repeated as Malta’s latest result without a date: the latest EEA profile available when this check was prepared reports 88.5% for 2025.

## Local pollution and wastewater compliance

The Environmental Health Directorate documented a temporary warning and closure at Balluta Bay sites B08 and B09 in 2024. Its report records microbial contamination, foul water from a storm-water tunnel, and a warning covering the bay from 31 May. The warning was lifted on 12 August after three consecutive samples were below the relevant thresholds. This serious, local episode can coexist with a country-level multi-year classification; it does not change the 2023 national percentage.

Separately, the Court of Justice of the European Union found Malta in breach of urban wastewater treatment requirements for named agglomerations in Case C-304/23. That ruling concerns wastewater collection and treatment compliance. It does not classify the bathing sites, and does not demonstrate that the EEA’s 92% result was false. The Commission’s own Malta factsheet presents both points: the bathing-water result under its “Highlights” and inadequate urban wastewater treatment under “Pollution”.

The candidate also referred to a beach closure “hours after receiving a Blue Flag”. That timing has not been verified in a primary record and is not used here.

## Verdict

**Supported (high confidence), with a date caveat.** The EEA’s 2023 dataset directly confirms 80 excellent sites out of 87, matching the Commission’s rounded 92%. Later data show why the year should be made explicit: the excellent share was 88.5% in 2025. Neither the Balluta Bay episode nor the CJEU wastewater judgment contradicts the narrower 2023 statistic, though both are relevant to a broader discussion of pollution and wastewater management.

This verdict assesses only the sentence quoted above. It does not establish that every bathing site is always free from pollution, or that Malta’s marine environment is in excellent overall condition.
