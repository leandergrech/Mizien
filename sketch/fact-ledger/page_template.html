Pre-check, on four test sentences

What a new claim would meet before any research starts. The four sentences were written for this test; nobody said them. The matcher uses no language model: a small word list maps words to concepts, numbers are compared with fact values, and direction (rise or fall) must agree.

What this test taught the design

  1. A fact's key needs the measure, not just the series. The first run of the consistency rule flagged 14 "conflicts", nearly all per-person against total changes from the same series. Adding a measure column left 3, all real.
  2. References must be checked by machine. 45 of the 57 check-row references I typed by hand pointed at the wrong row, mostly off by one line. The build caught every one.
  3. Automatic fixes need a person. The script suggested a row for each broken reference; 8 of 45 suggestions were wrong (a row with the same number but a different meaning, such as "+41 vs +11" instead of "+41% vs 2005").
  4. The basis is the most useful node. It explains why a ministry, a newspaper and an opposition party can all be right about the same country's emissions. It should be a first-class field on every emissions fact.
  5. Rows already overlap a lot. 37 facts carry 268 check rows for six reports. At about 6 facts and 9 links per report, the 57 reports would need roughly 350 facts and 530 links: in line with the 18–40 h estimate for that phase.
  6. Gaps become visible. Sub-claims with no linked fact (in italics in the graph) are either untested, rest on documents rather than data, or still need linking.
