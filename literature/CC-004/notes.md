## Gaps

- The ministry's 412 million kg figure has no stated scope (municipal only? commercial? BCRS?) or method
  (collected weight or recycled weight). Eurostat records 271 kt recycled or energy-recovered for 2020-24;
  2025 data are not yet published, so the five-year windows do not match exactly.
- The 141 to 95.5 million kg mixed-waste comparison has no start year. NSO/Eurostat do not publish "mixed
  waste" as a series; landfilled municipal waste fell 21% from 2019 to 2024.
- No peer-reviewed study of Malta's municipal waste system was found in Crossref searches.
- Resolved in v1.1: whether Malta notified a postponement (yes, December 2023; renounced November 2024), and why no
  composting or digestion is reported as recycling (treated biowaste used for landscaping and landfill remediation,
  reported as landfilled). Still open: tonnages of compost and digestate and where they go.

## Commission documents (read in full, 5 October 2026, v1.1)

Retrieved from the EU Publications Office (Cellar), e.g. `http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/52023DC0304`
with `Accept: application/pdf` (EUR-Lex itself shows a WAF challenge to scripts). Page numbers are the printed
page numbers (PDF page minus one for the COM and SWD(2023); the "Malta N" running numbers for SWD(2025) 318).
Short phrases below are verbatim.

**COM(2023) 304 final, early-warning report (data: municipal 2020, packaging 2019; information to April 2022)**

- p. 3: Malta is one of "10 Member States ... at risk of missing both the targets for municipal and all packaging
  waste for 2025" (with Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Greece, Hungary, Lithuania, Poland, Romania, Slovakia).
- p. 5: one of 18 states at risk of missing the 55% municipal target.
- p. 7: one of 10 states at risk of missing the 65% packaging target; Malta appears in all six material-specific
  rows (plastics, glass, aluminium, ferrous metal, paper and cardboard, wood); only Croatia and Malta are in all six.
- p. 10: one of 13 states "still far from the target of a maximum of 10% of landfilling of municipal waste to be
  reached by 2035".
- p. 11: rules allow some states "to postpone compliance beyond the target years, under specific conditions"; the
  Commission says this "should be considered with care".

**SWD(2023) 195 final, the early warning report for Malta**

- p. 1: 2020 municipal recycling rate "(including composting)" reported as 10.5% and landfill rate 82.5%; packaging
  40% in 2020. (Eurostat now shows 10.9% for 2020: later revision.)
- p. 2: capture rates 40% (dry recyclables) and 34% (biowaste); "limited use of compost (i.e. compost is used for
  landscaping and landfill remediation instead of recycling for agricultural purposes)"; recent actions "not yet
  resulted in measurable effects".
- p. 3: recommends that compost and digestate "should be used for land improvement and as fertiliser"; "Malta should
  aim to double its capture rate".

**SWD(2025) 318 final, 2025 Environmental Implementation Review, country report Malta (7.7.2025)**

- p. 3: "Malta missed by a large margin the 2020 target to recycle 50 % of its municipal waste and is on a course to
  miss the 2025 targets to recycle 55 % of municipal waste and 65 % of all packaging waste."
- p. 5: deposit-return scheme collection rates 83% (PET), 82% (aluminium cans), 74% (glass bottles), citing the
  scheme's website (no year given).
- p. 8: 11 states, "not including Malta", used the postponement option for 2025. "In December 2023, Malta notified the
  Commission of its intention to postpone the municipal waste and packaging waste targets for plastic and
  paper/cardboard." The Commission found the preconditions for the packaging postponement "were not complied with"
  and "did not accept the postponement". "In November 2024, Malta notified the Commission of its intention to renounce
  to its right of postponement", confirming its commitment to the targets in WFD Art. 11.2 and PPWD Art. 6.1.
  (That the 2025 deadline therefore applies in full is our reading.)
- p. 8: new MMRF (June 2023), MRF (March 2023), automated glass-sorting line (2024); Ecohive complex.
- p. 9: 2022 municipal rate 13% (Eurostat now 12.5%). "This recycling rate is entirely driven by materials recycling,
  as biowaste after treatment is used for landscaping and landfill remediation (20) and is consequently reported as
  landfilled." Footnote 20: WasteServ 2022; EEA early-warning assessment for Malta, 2022.
- p. 9 (figure notes): Malta "is finding it particularly challenging to obtain accurate data on the actual amount of
  waste entering the recycling process due to its reliance on exporting waste for recycling".
- p. 10: packaging rate 32% in 2022; annual fluctuations because Malta "relies on exports for recycling and keeps
  sorted waste in temporary storage until there is enough to export".
- p. 11: regional collection since 1 January 2023; biowaste collected door to door since 2018, but "the treatment of
  organic waste in Malta does not qualify as recycling"; deposit-return system launched November 2022: "Data for 2023
  indicate that 78 % of the beverage containers placed on the market were collected ... while the recycling rate was
  74 %" (citing ERA 2024); no landfill or incineration tax; landfill gate fee for mixed waste rising from EUR 40 to
  EUR 120 a tonne, 2023-2027; "Malta has not made progress in developing sufficient infrastructure for the recycling
  of waste, particularly biowaste."

## EU-27 comparison (v1.1)

Eurostat cei_wm011 for all states, retrieved 5 October 2026 (dataset updated 30 March 2026), in
`data/cc-004/eurostat_cei_wm011_eu27.csv` with flags. 20 states have both 2019 and 2024; AT, BG, CZ, EL, IE, IT and RO
have no 2024 value yet. Malta +7.6 points (9.1% to 16.7%) is 4th of 20, after SK +12.2, LV +11.7, LU +8.4 (LU values
flagged e). Malta's 2024 rate is 2nd-lowest of the 20 (CY 15.8%, flagged p). Packaging (cei_wm020, retrieved 2 Oct):
30.8% (2022) to 43.4% (2023); plastic 13.8% to 31.9%; glass 43.3% to 65.2%. The link to the deposit refund scheme
(launched November 2022) is our inference from timing.
