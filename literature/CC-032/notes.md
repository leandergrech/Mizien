## (a) "First of its kind in Europe": what we found

- The units are SmartFlowers: the manufacturer's news of 14 Nov 2025 reports "the installation of fifteen SmartFlowers at
  the Gozo Multi-Modal Hub in Ta' Xħajma" and calls it "one of the largest installations in Europe"; its 2025 review
  (29 Dec 2025): "one of Europe's largest installations". The maker quotes Camilleri as "By installing these
  [SmartFlowers] in Europe ..." (its square brackets replace "first solar flowers").
- SmartFlower is Austrian: "manufactured in Austria" (FAQ); "founded in Austria", acquired by EMI (Boston) in 2018
  (company page); "Austria's SmartFlower", "100% Austrian-made", over 1,000 units sold "in Europe, Asia, the Middle East
  and other regions" (pv magazine, 13 Jul 2016; company's figure).
- Dated European records before 21 Oct 2025 (each read in full): **Moneycab, 19 May 2015** (published 05:55 UTC; text
  credited "Umwelt Arena/mc/ps"): "Neu schmücken zwei riesige 'Sonnenblumen' die Umwelt Arena Spreitenbach", the
  smartflower all-in-one solar system, headline "In der Umwelt Arena erzeugen sogar Blumen Elektrizität" (two units
  newly installed in Switzerland; a visitor attraction, the arena's own text); **energynews.es, 3 Jun 2015** (page
  dated 03/06/2015; metadata 2 Jun 22:03 UTC): a **product launch, not an installation** ("Ayer se presentó en Madrid",
  "El producto ya se comercializa en España desde hoy mismo"; "se ha instalado en España" is a figure of speech for
  arriving on the market); **smartgridsinfo.es, 28 Jan 2016**: Smartflower presented "smartflower POP-e", an all-in-one
  PV unit with an EV charging station (up to 22 kW for e-bikes and cars), a "tarjeta de visita verde" for public
  administrations and companies (a product, not a dated installation); ASFINAG motorway rest area, Hinterbrühl,
  Austria, in operation by 7 Dec 2016, 50% of the lighting's electricity, EUR 19,000 (MeinBezirk.at: a
  Regionauten-Community post by Roland Weber with an ASFINAG photo, not newsroom news; corroborated by the IÖB project
  page); Vodafone, Newbury, UK, Aug 2021 (Newbury Today). The verdict rests on the 2016 ASFINAG and 2021 Vodafone
  records and the maker's own wording; Dec 2016 is the earliest in-service date. Undated dealer lists: 17
  UK and Jersey installations including triples and doubles (Green Mole); Denmark, Spain, Scotland (Smartflower
  Benelux). See `data/cc-032/european_records.csv`.
- Use: the maker presented an EV-charging version (POP-e) in Spain on 28 Jan 2016, aimed at public administrations
  and companies. We found no earlier European flower array charging buses specifically, but we did not search for
  that beyond the pages and searches listed below, so the report says only that nothing we found shows one.
- What "first" could mean: (1) first solar flowers in Europe: contradicted by the records above; (2) first installation
  of its kind (a public body powering transport infrastructure): the 2016 ASFINAG pilot is a public transport-
  infrastructure use in Europe, though for lighting rather than bus charging, and the 2016 POP-e was pitched at
  public administrations (only buses specifically might be new); (3) first or largest by number: the
  maker says "one of the largest", not the largest; the minister's own quote says second in the world by number at one
  site, which we could not test; (4) first in Malta: the minister's own quoted words; consistent with the PQ answer
  (second-hand) that no other such installation exists in Malta; a web search (6 Oct 2026) found no earlier Maltese
  SmartFlower.

## (b) "Power the charging of electric buses": what we found

- Capacity of the Gozo units: **not published** in any source read (TVM, Lovin Malta, EC page, maker's two Gozo pages,
  Newsbook PQ report, TED notice). The maker's current unit is 2.5 kWp (data sheet Rev G 2019; pv magazine Australia
  2024), so 15 units = 37.5 kWp if they are that model.
- Output: PVGIS 5.3 (SARAH3 2005-2023, 14% default loss) at the Park and Ride: 85,121 kWh a year with two-axis tracking
  (5,675 kWh per unit; 233 kWh a day on average; 152 in December, 315 in July); 62,341 kWh fixed at the optimal 32
  degrees: +36.5%. Maker's range for 15 units: 60,000-97,500 kWh a year. **No metered output has been published.**
- Buses: six MGP-owned electric buses run the Park and Ride every 10 minutes (GRDA, Dec 2025), operated by Malta Public
  Transport under negotiated contracts (TED). **Their energy use, battery size, model and operating hours are not
  published** (searched 6 Oct 2026: TVM, MPT routes and electric-bus pages, TED notices 2017-2026, GRDA, Newsbook, The
  Shift (2021, 2025), Lovin Malta (2021), GuideMeMalta (2021, 10-minute service 5-8 am and 3-6 pm at launch)).
- Timing: MPT's page says "Primarily charging overnight", with fast-charging options at the depot ensuring service
  during the day; the Ta' Xħajma depot, in service since May 2026 (seven months after the statement), charges the
  route fleet during the night (TVM 28 May 2026); where and when the six shuttle buses charge is not published; the flowers produce only in daylight and
  close at sundown (EC page). So they can feed daytime charging or other loads directly and offset night charging on a
  yearly balance through the grid, as the statement says ("offset"); powering night charging directly would need
  storage, and none is mentioned. How the flowers are wired (to the chargers, the hub or the grid) is not published.
- Scale check (a conditional order of magnitude, kept only in report section 5; consumption from other routes and
  buses, hours not published, so the share covered cannot be calculated and the figure is not used in any rating,
  tile or the flyer): 8.5 km round trip (OSRM), six an hour = 51 km per
  hour of service; at 1.45-2.1 kWh/km (Li et al. 2025, simulated; Czapla and Sierpiński 2023, modelled) that is 74-107
  kWh per service hour, so the flowers' average day equals about 2.2-3.2 hours of 10-minute service (1.4-2.1 h in
  December). Whether they cover all the shuttle's charging cannot be calculated without its hours and consumption.
- PQ answers (Newsbook, 19 Jan 2026; **second-hand**): no technical report, feasibility study or value-for-money
  comparison was made; viability could not be compared because no other such installation exists in Malta; all the
  flowers were in working order.

## (c) Cost and funding

- Stated: about EUR 850,000, RRP C1-I5 (TVM, EC page). PQ answer: EUR 699,611.98 excl. VAT, public call, three bids
  (Newsbook, second-hand). TED award notice 742398-2024: CT3013/2024 "dual axis solar photovoltaic system for the Public
  Works Department", concluded 3 Dec 2024, Alternative Technologies Ltd, EUR 718,830.07 (estimated 774,376); **our
  identification**, the notice names no site. With VAT at 18% (EPRS): PQ figure EUR 825,542; TED figure EUR 848,219
  (close to the stated EUR 850,000). The EUR 19,218 difference is not explained.
- C1-I5 target (Council 11894/26 ADD 1): 143 kWp of PV in public spaces by Q2 2026; 37.5 kWp would be 26%. The measure
  was amended for supply-chain delays (Council 15652/25).
