# CC-006 evidence notes

Research cut-off: 2 October 2026. The check assesses whether Malta's 2026 spring-hunting derogation met the Birds Directive's “small numbers” and strict-supervision conditions. The original candidate also paraphrased the Ornis Committee recommendation; no 2026 committee minutes were listed on WBRU's archive (the archive listed minutes through 2024). The contemporaneous MaltaToday account is retained as a lead, not treated as a verbatim primary source.

## Primary wording and legal figures

Article 9(1)(c) of Directive 2009/147/EC permits derogations only “under strictly supervised conditions and on a selective basis” and for birds “in small numbers”. This exact primary wording is preserved in `claims/CC-006/claim.yml` and was checked on EUR-Lex on 2 October 2026. Regulations 3–4 of Legal Notices 80 and 81 define “small numbers” as “a sample in the order of one percent (1%) of the total annual mortality of the population in question”. The PDFs were opened through their official Legislation Malta records and read in the embedded PDF viewer on 2 October 2026. Each notice explains that the relevant migratory population is the population of EU regions from which the main contingents passing through Malta originate.

| Species | 1% annual-mortality benchmark in notice | 2026 national quota | Quota as share of mortality | Quota as share of 1% benchmark |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Common Quail | 2,416 of 241,638 | 2,400 | 0.993% | 99.3% |
| European Turtle-dove | 2,510 of 251,032 | 1,500 | 0.598% | 59.8% |

Calculations use the rounded integer benchmarks printed in the legislation and are reproducible with `data/cc-006/calc.py`; input values and formulas are recorded in `data/cc-006/checks.csv`. Quota-to-benchmark ratios are comparisons with the legal model, not observed harvest or independent proof of population impact. The relevant population definition is not Malta's resident population.

L.N. 80/2026 sets the Quail period as 13 April–3 May 2026, with the overall bag limit set at 2,400 birds. L.N. 81/2026 sets the Turtle-dove period as 20 April–3 May 2026, with an overall bag limit of 1,500. Both provide for hunting from two hours before sunrise until noon and allow the Minister to terminate the season by notice. These are primary legal facts; they do not establish the contents of the Ornis vote.

## Conservation status and scientific literature

WBRU's March 2026 assessment reports an 11% Pan-European Turtle-dove decline over 2015–2024 and an 88% decline since 1980; it says the central-eastern flyway continues to decline. It also states that the most recent EU Article 12 submissions were provisional and not yet adopted. These trends make it especially important to distinguish a legal mortality benchmark from a forecast of sustainable harvest.

Caruana-Galizia and Fenech (2016) estimate historical spring-hunting impacts using hunter records and independent harvest estimates. Their independent estimates concern 1980–1992 and depend on older population denominators with wide error margins. They are not used to infer 2026 quota impacts. Publisher metadata and DOI were checked against Cambridge University Press; an open repository full text was reviewed. No full text is committed here.

## Supervision evidence and limits

The WBRU page listed its 2025 outcome report as the latest spring-season outcome report on 2 October 2026. For 2025 it records 1,336 patrols/field inspections, 909 hunter spot-checks and 19 detected offences. The enforcement figure is a count of activity, not a measure of deterrence or full compliance. The 2025 report cannot establish what happened in 2026.

The 2026 Legal Notices require compliance with the framework and Special Spring Hunting Licence conditions. The April 2026 Government Gazette contains a separate Turtle-dove research-capture derogation with licence controls; it must not be confused with recreational hunting. FKNK's 7 April statement says it would conduct a survey to estimate a “sustainability index” using voluntary member participation and records of sex of harvested birds. That survey announcement is not an enforcement report or independent outcome assessment.

BirdLife Malta published field-monitoring accounts during and after the 2026 season, including its estimate that 72% of hunters it observed in the first week were targeting Turtle-doves before the Turtle-dove season opened. This is an NGO estimate based on observations and should be read as stakeholder monitoring, not an official enforcement count. The WBRU archive did not list a 2026 outcome report at the research cut-off. Accordingly, the statutory controls are documented, but whether 2026 hunting was “strictly supervised” in practice cannot yet be established from the official outcome data.
