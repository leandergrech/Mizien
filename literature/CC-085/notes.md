# CC-085 notes (10 Oct 2026)

## The wording

- **Tony Zahra, President, MHRA**, signed column in *Horeca Malta*, 23 Dec 2022 (read in full via the site's WordPress API, post 5110):
  the carrying capacity report started from the beds operating and "the applications submitted for new beds which have been approved
  or are in the course of being approved", and "the Deloitte report concluded that Malta will need to attract 4.7 million tourists each
  spending an average of 7 nights in Malta oi reach 80 percent occupancy throughout the year" ("oi" is printed for "to"). He adds that
  the Tourism Minister remarked that the government is not expecting 4.7 million (indirect speech, not rated).
- **Tony Zahra** to *The Malta Independent on Sunday*, reprinted by *The Malta Business Weekly*, 28 Nov 2024 (direct quote):
  "If we want the 80% occupancy rate of hotels we had in 2019, with the number of beds we have, we need 4.7 million tourists. The next
  thing one has to ask is whether the country can sustain 4.7 million tourists?" The original *Independent* page was not read (403).
- The claim record's locator (*Newsbook*, John Paul Cordina, 22 Sep 2022) and the other launch reports (*Malta Business Weekly*,
  *Business Now*, 22 Sep 2022) give 4.7 million in their own words, with no MHRA wording in quotation marks. *MaltaToday*

## The MHRA's own documents (full text read)

- **Deloitte for MHRA, Carrying Capacity Study for Tourism in the Maltese islands**, final report dated 4 July 2022 (99 pp., on
  mhra.org.mt since Sep 2022). Two calculations of the arrivals needed:
  - p. 27 (bed stock): collective beds 38,000 (+80/90/100%) at 76.7% occupancy, private rented 23,502 (+25/30/35%) at 59.3%, divided
    by the 2019 stay (labelled 7; the arithmetic uses 7.024 = 19,338,860 / 2,753,240): 3.63 / 3.82 / 4.01 million rented-accommodation
    arrivals; with 2019's 513,921 tourists in non-rented homes these are the launch slides' 4.1 / 4.3 / 4.5 million (slide 11,
    "arrivals required for FY19 occupancy").
  - p. 66 (airport connectivity): 2019 guest nights grown by 60 / 70 / 80%, at 7 nights: **4,405,184 / 4,680,509 / 4,955,833**
    arrivals. 4,680,509 is the 4.7 million (Scenario 2). It equals 2,753,240 x 1.7.
  - The scenarios hold occupancy at 2019 levels. **No 80% occupancy condition** appears in the text layer of the report or the slides
    (pdftotext; every "occupan" hit listed and read, 10 Oct 2026); "80%" appears as a growth rate (Sc3 bed stock or guest nights, the
    collective stock's +80% in Sc1 of p. 27), as the ESF co-financing rate and in sewage and labour tables. Charts drawn as images were
    looked at for slide 11 and report pp. 27 and 37 only.
  - p. 25: open permits (approved and under review) imply about 35,000 more collective beds over 4-6 years; stakeholders agreed on
    +80% to +100% on 2019. A Planning Authority exercise on approved but unbuilt permits "was however not shared with us".
- **Deloitte for MHRA, Future proofing the Maltese tourism industry: Market update**, 24 Sep 2024 (78 pp.). Describes the original
  estimate as 4.5 million (pp. 4, 10, 17). MTA pipeline at end April 2024: 13,543 rooms, 27,672 beds, 483 establishments (p. 69).
  Arrivals needed at 2023 occupancy (NSO collective 65.8%, stay 4.7 nights): 4.4 million with the full pipeline and 15% of old beds
  retired, 4.8 million with none retired (pp. 9, 72-74). Hotel survey occupancy (p. 27, chart labels): 2019 5-star 72.7%, 4-star 81.2%.

## Literature (abstracts only; DOIs verified in Crossref)

- McCool & Lime (2001): numerical carrying capacities rest on assumptions rarely met; ask what conditions are acceptable rather than how
  many is too many. Relevance: 4.7 million is a bed-filling requirement, not a carrying capacity (the study itself says those numbers
  would not be desirable).
- Saveriades (2000): social carrying capacity of Cyprus east-coast resorts; thresholds beyond which residents' acceptance and visitor
  satisfaction decline. Island context.
- Dogru et al. (2020): in US hotel markets, more hotel supply lowers hotel occupancy (Airbnb supply lowers prices but not occupancy).
  Supports the mechanism: beds added faster than demand lower occupancy.
- Hung & Tsou (2025): static capacity models are limited for small islands; proposes an indicator framework.
- Not found: a peer-reviewed study of Malta's hotel bed pipeline or of the arrivals needed to fill it. Searched Crossref
  ("tourism carrying capacity Malta", "overtourism Maltese Islands", "Malta tourism hotel occupancy supply") and OpenAlex (429, rate
  limit) on 10 Oct 2026. Briguglio and Avellino's paper on over-tourism in Malta (cited by ADPD, 2022) was not located or read.
