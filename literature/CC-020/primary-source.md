# CC-020 primary source

**Document:** Hjerp, P. and Coffey, C. (Ecocentric Consulting), *Analysing Malta's implementation of Directive
2002/49/EC on the Assessment and Management of Environmental Noise*. Study requested by the European Parliament's
Committee on Petitions (PETI); Policy Department for Citizens, Equality and Culture. PE 783.089, February 2026.
PDF ISBN 978-92-848-3381-8, doi:10.2861/6278624 (resolves to the EU Publications Office record; not in Crossref).
**Copy:** supplied by the maintainer (local PDF, not committed). Read 4 Oct 2026.
**Status of the text:** the opinions are the authors', not the European Parliament's official position (study disclaimer).

## Verbatim passages (short extracts)

- Chapter 3 key findings: "Legal transposition of the END into national legislation is not considered to be the
  cause of the noise problems in Malta." Malta's 2024 legislation transposes the Directive "with just two issues
  of non-conformity that are meaningful" (Art. 8(3); Annex V and limit values under Art. 5).
- Chapter 4 (section 4.1): "There have been no END infringement proceedings against Malta between 2015 and 2025."
- Chapter 6 key findings: "A 2019 Maltese survey showed that 21% of participants considered noise pollution to be
  among the four most important environmental issues for them, compared to an EU28 average of 9%." (The study cites
  ERA 2023c for this figure. Checked 6 Oct 2026: the annex is readable via Wayback and carries the figure on pp. 5-6
  in the Office of the Superintendent of Public Health's comment, naming no survey; the Marmara Oct 2019 summary
  does not contain it. Origin unidentified.)
- Chapter 6: "The transposition and implementation of the END evidently is not the cause of the noise problems in
  Malta."
- Conclusions: the petition's sectors "largely fall outside of the scope of the END", so the problems it sets out
  "do not significantly reflect a failure in the implementation or enforcement of the END".

## What to check
Whether the transposition table and the infringement record support the conclusion; whether the 21% vs 9% figure
is reported correctly from its source; and how END-scope exposure (EEA, ERA noise maps) compares with WHO guideline
levels. Read alongside CC-104 (PN petition: Malta fails the noise law).
