## The passages about Malta's 2030 effort-sharing target

Chapter 3.2, *Progress towards effort sharing targets*, "Emission reduction targets for 2030", p. 30:

> The above findings are confirmed by Member States’ latest projections of March 2025 (see Figure 13). Prior to any
> use of ESR flexibilities by Member States to meet their targets, Germany, Ireland and Malta show the largest
> projected gaps in 2030 whereas Bulgaria, Greece and Portugal show the largest overachievement of their 2030 targets.

Note under Figure 13 ("Projected change of ESR emissions and gap to target in 2030 (%)"), p. 30:

> Note: Malta’s gap to target is 49 and 61 percentage points, exceeding its 19% reduction target. This means Malta is
> projected to emit more in 2030 than in 2005.

(Figure 13 plots, for each Member State, the projected change 2005-2030 "with additional measures" and "with existing
measures", the gap to target under each, and the target. The note does not say which number belongs to which
scenario; Figure 13 and the staff working document's Table 25, p. 114, show 49 with additional and 61 with existing
measures.)

Same section, "Emission reductions in 2021-2030", p. 31:

> The Commission finds that 10 Member States would still exceed their limits in at least one year over the period
> 2021-2030. Cyprus, Croatia, Italy and Romania would already have excess emissions in the first compliance period
> (2021-2025), while Austria, Estonia, Germany, Malta, Ireland and Sweden are projected to have excess emissions in
> the second compliance period (2026-2030).

The assumptions behind that sentence (p. 31): the Commission "assumes that Member States will implement their
additional measures as included in their projections ‘with additional measures’ (WAM)", that they bank and borrow, and
"that the Member States that notified their intention to use ETS flexibility will do so when needed".

The Commission's own conclusion, same page:

> Given the size of the projected ESR surplus and the possibilities to trade surpluses under both the ESR and LULUCF
> Regulation, the Commission cannot, at this stage, conclude that Member States are not making sufficient progress to
> meet their ESR obligations.

## What the claim record says, and what the Commission said

The claim record's text ("Malta is projected to emit more in 2030 than in 2005 in effort-sharing sectors, missing its
target") is the intake's paraphrase. "Projected to emit more in 2030 than in 2005" is the Commission's wording. "Missing
its target" is not: the Commission speaks of a "gap to target" and of "excess emissions", in both places conditioned
on the use of flexibilities, and says it cannot yet conclude that progress is insufficient. The check rates the
Commission's words and treats "missing its target" as the intake's wording (not rated).
