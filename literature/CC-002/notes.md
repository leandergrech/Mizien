## Synthesis and limits

- A 10:1 planting ratio is an input count. It is not a measured estimate of how many replacements survive or of the time needed to provide comparable habitat structure and ecological function.
- Tree transplantation can impose physiological stress even when trees are pruned, irrigated and fertilised. The olive study is useful mechanistic evidence but cannot be converted into an expected Comino mortality rate.
- Plant translocation outcomes vary substantially by species, source, site suitability, technique and aftercare. The Italian database has incomplete survival reporting and concerns conservation translocations across a range of plant life forms, not this permit's 348 specimens.
- The Comino flora inventory supports the island's botanical sensitivity and the need to distinguish alien, invasive, endemic, protected and planted specimens. It does not map these specific 678 removal specimens.
- The ecological case for removing alien plants is context-specific. The Azores study cannot establish benefits at the Comino hotel sites; the local species and restoration target must be stated and measured.

## Gaps

- Obtain and inspect the complete EP 1472/22 species schedule, tree condition data, method statements and compensation planting plan from the public ERIS record. The public release does not provide the full inventory.
- Resolve ERA's 467-oleander count (11 Aug) versus MaltaToday's 468 (8 Aug), which quotes the case-officer report.
- Confirm species identities, native status, origin, age/size, condition, transplant location, planting stock provenance and aftercare for each intervention.
- Publish survival and condition at years 1, 3 and 5, plus vegetation/habitat indicators at removal and compensation sites. Record losses and replacements separately.
- A site-specific ecological assessment or before-after/control-impact monitoring would be required to demonstrate net restoration or functional compensation.
