# Evidence grades

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Controlled or randomised experiment |
| **B** | Observational study with a control or gradient |
| **C** | Review, professional guidance or official statistics summary |
| **D** | Assertion or anecdote with no data |

Where studies disagree, the report shows both sides, says why they may differ (species, climate, design, sample) and
states what it means for the claim. We do not average disagreement away.

## Source order

1. Peer-reviewed literature and systematic reviews.
2. Independent datasets (Eurostat, EEA, NSO, WHO, Copernicus, national monitoring networks).
3. Regulator and EU reports (for example the Commission's Environmental Implementation Review).
4. News reports and advocacy material: used to find leads and quote what was said, never as the only evidence for a finding.

## Second-hand sources

A study known only through another paper's summary is marked as second-hand (◆ in the PDF reports) and is not
presented as having been read.
