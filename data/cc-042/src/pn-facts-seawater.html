The hidden truths about Malta’s degrading bathing water and beaches

They polluted the sea.

The story of poor bathing water and beach closures in Malta.

Government’s official data shows bathing closures linked to sewage, foul water, sewer overflow, drainage faults, runoff and faecal-indicator bacteria. After a disastrous summer in 2025, this year’s season has opened with poor water classifications and a new sewage warning.

What E. coli means

E. coli is not a political word. It is an official warning sign of faecal contamination.

The European Environment Agency says E. coli and intestinal enterococci are “important indicators of faecal contamination” in bathing water. Some E. coli strains can cause severe disease, and European health authorities warn that dangerous E. coli infections can lead to a life-threatening kidney complication.

The point is direct: faecal contamination is a public-health warning sign, not just an ugly headline.

Sources: EEA bathing-water explainer; WHO E. coli fact sheet; ECDC E. coli disease information.

Miriam Dalli’s failed promise

Sewage warning four weeks after Minister Miriam Dalli promised that repairs were complete.

On 12 May 2026, Minister Minister Dalli said Pretty Bay drainage works were “completed in time” for summer and that the area was “better equipped to serve residents, businesses and visitors for many years to come”. But, on 8 June, the public was told not to bathe nearby because of “percolation of sewage into the sea”.

Sources: DOI / Environment Ministry, 12 May 2026; DOI Environmental Health Directorate, 8 June 2026.
2026: this is not over

The new season opened with poor sites and a closure.

The 2026 reports cannot be brushed aside as last year’s problem. In the first three published weeks, Government’s water monitoring website already shows repeated poor ratings at Balluta Bay and early poor or sufficient ratings at nearby central-coast bathing areas. Week 2 also recorded a health warning for Exiles Bay.

Official clarification

“Poor” is not a beach review. It is a bacteria-based official classification.

The 2026 EHD report states that site classification is based on intestinal enterococci and Escherichia coli. It also explains that a poor rating indicates consistent higher-than-normal results close to threshold limits and is calculated from the samples collected from the site.

EHD Site Classification Week 3, 2026
Already found in breach

Malta had already lost the wastewater case.

In October 2024, the Court of Justice of the European Union ruled against Malta over urban wastewater treatment. The judgment says Malta failed to take the necessary measures to ensure wastewater from Malta South and Malta North was properly treated before discharge, and failed on monitoring obligations.

So when 2025 closure files later recorded sewage, foul water, sewer overflow and faecal-indicator bacteria at bathing sites, this was not happening in a vacuum.

Sources: CJEU judgment, Commission v Malta, Case C-304/23; European Commission referral note.

The bottom line

People are being asked to trust the sea. The official files say the sea has been repeatedly closed.

The strongest evidence is not a slogan. It is in government’s own paperwork: closures, sewage warnings, foul-water reports, daily samples, E. coli, intestinal enterococci, and repeated problem sites at the very start of the 2026 bathing season.

Public-health context: EHD says bathing is only not recommended in closed sites; the EEA says E. coli and intestinal enterococci are indicators of faecal contamination. Links are listed below.

Action taken

The PN has been acting to stop this abuse!

9 Jul 2025

Raised directly with the European Commissioner.

MEP Peter Agius met Environment Commissioner Jessika Roswall and pressed for full implementation of EU environmental standards in Malta, citing repeated beach closures caused by sewage spills.

18 Jul 2025

Called for independent seawater testing.

He formally called on Commission services to consider an independent audit and sampling of Malta’s seawater quality after the removal of sensitive-area designations near sewage-treatment plants.

2026 PETI

Took citizens’ petition to Europe.

The European Parliament’s Petitions Committee heard the Malta sewage petition; reports say the Commission representative indicated that latest readings still pointed to continued breach of EU rules.

Sources: EPP, 9 July 2025; EPP, 18 July 2025; MaltaToday, 24 March 2026; European Parliament petition 1172/2025.
